# Original translation proposals: explicitly unverified

14 September 2026. These hypotheses were formulated in this exchange, not presented as recovered meanings or accepted translations. Literature priority has not been established. They must not be treated as findings of phases 1-5. No independent semantic prediction has validated either proposal.

## H1: DA-DU-MA-TA as an adjusted assessment

**Proposed functional gloss:** assessment after adjustment / reassessed allotment.

**Proposed translation of HT95a:**

> Reassessed grain allotment: DA-ME 10; MI-NU-TE 10; SA-RU 20; KU-NI-SU 10; DI-DE-RU 10; QE-RA2-U 7.

The labels are retained, not translated as particular places, persons, or grain varieties. Grain is the conventional interpretation of GRA. The original word need not have encoded all three English words in the functional gloss.

### Evidence and assumptions

HT95a has heading DA-DU-MA-TA followed by GRA. HT95b has heading A-DU and six corresponding readable entries, each 10. Under the published general assessment interpretation of this account, I hypothesize that the uniform schedule on b is a reference assessment and a is an adjusted schedule. This direction is a hypothesis; face labels are not dates.

| Entry | HT95b | HT95a | a minus b |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DA-ME | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| MI-NU-TE | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| SA-RU | 10 | 20 | +10 |
| KU-NI-SU | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| DI-DE-RU | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| QE-RA2-U | 10 | 7 | -3 |

The six shared entries sum to 60 and 67 respectively. These are calculated subtotals, not numerals inscribed as totals. A damaged interval on b prevents treating this as an independently certified exhaustive face total. The comparison assumes corresponding commodities/units across faces; GRA is explicit only on a. Some name-sign readings on a are doubtful in the commentary.

**What is original in this proposal:** assigning the contrast between the two headings to reference versus adjusted assessment. The inscription, its repeated entries, and the general assessment interpretation are already published.

A uniform unit conversion cannot account for ratios 1, 2, and 0.7 simultaneously. An unqualified one-sided reduction from the b schedule also fails because SA-RU increases. This does not decide among reassessment, actual receipts against assessment, separate named accounts, or commodity-specific schedules. In particular, nothing yet shows that DA-DU-MA-TA is a common noun rather than a name.

**Testable consequences:** a further independently readable occurrence should behave like an accounting-state heading; a bilingual or otherwise independently anchored occurrence as a personal/commodity name would undermine this gloss. A second paired account with unambiguous reference/adjusted states would be much stronger evidence. There is only one exact occurrence in the pinned corpus, so this condition has not been tested successfully.

## H2: initial A as an administrative-responsibility relation in selected name pairs

**Proposed functional relation:** A-X means on X's account / under X's responsibility, whereas bare X names the entity without that relation.

This is a proposed interpretation of specific candidate pairs, not permission to remove A from every word beginning with A. The exact grammar (prefix, written-together element, or something else) is not established. 'Account' is an English rendering of the proposed administrative relation, not a separately decoded noun.

### New proposed readings

HT96b, A-PA-RA-NE:

> Under Parane's responsibility.

The following QA-*118-RA-RE and *516 remain uninterpreted. The associated commodity list conventionally reads grain 40+J+E, OLE+U 4, FIC 2+K. J is doubtful in the commentary. No particular oil grade, grain variety, or unit is invented.

ZA10a, selected noncontiguous entries:

> Tanate: 2. ... On Tanate's account: 1.

This corresponds to TA-NA-TE 2 and A-TA-NA-TE 1. The ellipsis stands for intervening entries. It is not a continuous ancient sentence. What is counted is not established by these entries; no claim about one versus two grammatical persons follows.

### Motivation and weaknesses

PA-RA-NE appears as an entry on HT115a/b; A-PA-RA-NE appears in heading material on HT96a/b. Both objects are assigned to HT Scribe 8. This motivates a relation between an entity and an account administered by or assigned to it, rather than a purely regional spelling difference. HT115 is a palimpsest; the 1 by PA-RA-NE on its a face is doubtful. The b-face PA-RA-NE with J provides the less qualified entry comparison, though preceding text is broken.

The same-object TA-NA-TE / A-TA-NA-TE contrast on ZA10 permits a test beyond heading position: the proposed relation could appear in an ordinary line entry. It does not prove that the two strings designate the same entity. The example was already known when this hypothesis was formed, so it is not a held-out prediction.

**What is original in this proposal:** the specific administrative-responsibility interpretation and its English applications. The shorter/longer forms and scribal assignments are known. These observations do not select responsibility over ownership, origin, ordinary case marking, or two distinct names. No first-in-literature priority claim is made.

**Predictions:** further paired forms should show a consistent difference in administrative role, not necessarily in amount or heading position. A blanket rule A=heading is not proposed; the earlier KA-RU/A-KA-RU heading counterexample remains relevant. Independently anchored separate personal identities would undermine the same-entity analysis.

## What the calculations establish

A fresh run on the checksum-pinned corpus reproduces the six HT95 entry matches, their +10/-3 differences, one DA-DU-MA-TA occurrence, two PA-RA-NE occurrences, two A-PA-RA-NE occurrences, and one occurrence of each TANATE form. These checks verify the selected digital input and arithmetic. They do not verify either meaning.
