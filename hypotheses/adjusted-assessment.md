# H1: DA-DU-MA-TA as an adjusted assessment

[All hypotheses](README.md) | [Evidence register](../docs/evidence.md)

**Status: low-confidence translation hypothesis, weakened by its closest comparandum.** The proposed meaning has not been validated on an independent occurrence, and the one other tablet that carries HT 95's entry labels, HT 86, does not behave as the proposal predicts. Literature priority has not been established.

## Proposed translation

For the opening account on **HT 95a**:

> **Reassessed grain allotment:** DA-ME 10; MI-NU-TE 10; SA-RU 20; KU-NI-SU 10; DI-DE-RU 10; QE-RA2-U 7.

The claim is that `DA-DU-MA-TA` describes an **adjusted assessment or allotment**, rather than naming a person, place, institution, or commodity. The other labels remain untranslated. "Grain" comes from the conventional interpretation of the accompanying `GRA` logogram; it is not a newly decoded word. "Reassessed allotment" is a proposed functional gloss, not an analysis assigning separate meanings to each sign.

## Inscription and comparison

The published headings are:

```text
HT 95a:  DA-DU-MA-TA . GRA
HT 95b:  A-DU
```

The six corresponding readable entries are shown below. Their order is aligned by label for comparison, not reproduced as identical across the two faces. Some label-sign readings on face a are doubtful in the edition.[1]

| Entry | Face b | Face a | a minus b |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DA-ME | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| MI-NU-TE | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| SA-RU | 10 | 20 | +10 |
| KU-NI-SU | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| DI-DE-RU | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| QE-RA2-U | 10 | 7 | -3 |
| **Calculated subtotal of these entries** | **60** | **67** | **+7** |

The subtotals are calculated here, **not written as totals on the tablet**. A damaged interval on face b prevents certifying 60 as its exhaustive total. Grain is explicit only on face a, so the cross-face comparison assumes corresponding commodities and units. The labels "a" and "b" do not establish the order of writing.[1]

## HT 86: the comparandum the proposal must explain

Younger's HT 95 commentary, the source cited for H1, adds that "the same names (place names?) recur on HT 86". The [heading comparison](../docs/methods.md#heading-comparison) collects the six entry labels from HT 95 and searches the pinned corpus for them. Exactly four records carry two or more: the two faces of HT 95 and the two faces of HT 86. No other document carries more than one.[3]

HT 86a places the labels under **two headings on one face**, separated by a ruled line:

```text
HT 86a:  A-KA-RU .
         KU-NI-SU   GRA+K+L   20
         SA-RU                20
         DI-DE-RU             20
         QA-RA2-WA            10
         -----------------------
         A-DU .
         DA-ME      GRA+B     20
         MI-NU-TE             20
```

HT 86b repeats the first section (`A-KA-RU . KU-NI-SU GRA+K+L 20, SA-RU 20, DI-DE[`), which the edition marks as erased; Younger calls the tablet a double palimpsest (Schoep 2002, 79). Both HT 86 and HT 95 are treated in the commentary as probable assessments (Schoep 2002, 106).[1,3]

Three observations follow:

1. On HT 86a, `A-DU` heads two of the labels and `A-KA-RU` heads the other four, at the same amount under both headings. The A-DU section is not a reference schedule for the whole list.
2. Each section carries a different grain ligature (`GRA+K+L`, `GRA+B`). That reads as a difference of commodity or transaction between the sections, not of accounting state.
3. `A-DU` is not specific to HT 95. It occurs on ten records at three sites (HT 85a, HT 86a, HT 88, HT 92, HT 95b, HT 99a, HT 133, KH 11, KH 23, TY 3a), seven times as the first row, before grain, people, oil, and cyperus.

Under H1, face a of HT 95 revises face b. HT 86 shows the same labels partitioned under two headings, with no revision relation between the sections. A reading in which headings name sections or transaction types covers both tablets; H1 covers only HT 95. This does not refute H1, but it removes the reason to prefer it over that alternative.

The labels also occur singly elsewhere with other commodities: SA-RU with olives on HT 123+124a, MI-NU-TE with cyperus on HT 106, DA-ME with a grain ligature and 74 on HT 120. That bears on whether they name grain varieties or places, a question the comparison does not decide.[3]

## Reasoning behind the proposal

A uniform schedule on one face and selective increases and decreases on the other are consistent with a reference assessment and a reassessment. Under H1, the heading on face a identifies the adjusted accounting state. The existing general assessment interpretation is published: Younger's commentary cites Schoep 2002, p. 106.[1]

**What this project adds is the semantic assignment to the heading.** It does not discover the repeated labels or the general possibility of assessments. Under the same-unit assumption, a uniform conversion factor cannot explain ratios of 1, 2, and 0.7 simultaneously. A model allowing only reductions from face b also fails because SA-RU increases.

Those eliminations do not establish H1. The distinction between "reference" and "adjusted" is assumed, not independently read from either heading. No standalone dictionary meaning for `A-DU` follows, and its ten occurrences show it is a general heading term rather than one half of a pair.

## Competing explanations

| Alternative | Why it remains live |
|---|---|
| Two separately named accounts | Nothing independently identifies `DA-DU-MA-TA` as a common noun rather than a name. |
| Section or transaction headings over a fixed list | HT 86a heads subsets of the same list with `A-KA-RU` and `A-DU` at equal amounts, and `A-DU` heads ten records. |
| Assessment versus actual receipts | The differences could concern fulfillment rather than revision of an assessment. |
| Different commodities, rates, or periods | Shared labels need not imply an otherwise identical accounting context; HT 86a's sections carry different grain ligatures. |
| A different direction of revision | Face labels are not dates; the uniform list need not precede the other. |

## What would discriminate between them?

An independently interpretable use as a personal or commodity name would undermine this gloss. Another readable occurrence of `DA-DU-MA-TA` as an accounting-state heading, especially in an independently identified reference/revision pair, would strengthen it. The pinned corpus has one exact occurrence, and its nearest comparandum, HT 86, is a partition rather than a revision.[2,3]

The observed +10 and -3 differences are arithmetic evidence about the list. They are not a translation test. No statistical result in this repository validates the proposed word meaning.
